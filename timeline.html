2027 Ballot Roadmap

The path from here to a Cedar Rapids municipal utility referendum, what has to happen and when.

Completed
In Progress, We Are Here
Upcoming
Phase 1 : August 2026

Campaign Launch & Community Organizing

Establish the campaign, build initial coalition, connect with allied organizations (including Save Morgan Valley), begin community education efforts, and launch this website.

Phase 2 : September 2026 – Spring 2027

Petition Drive IN PROGRESS

Launch the formal petition drive across Cedar Rapids neighborhoods. Target: verified signatures from Cedar Rapids residents well above the threshold required to trigger a ballot referendum under Iowa Code and the Cedar Rapids City Charter. Door-to-door canvassing, tabling at community events, neighborhood association outreach.

Phase 3 : Spring 2027

Petition Submission & Council Engagement

Submit completed petition to the City. Present the full research package, rate data, feasibility analysis, and legal framework to the Cedar Rapids City Council. Request placement of a municipal utility referendum on the November 2027 ballot. Engage council members publicly and in testimony.

Phase 4 : Summer–Fall 2027

Public Campaign for the Referendum

If the referendum is placed on the ballot: run a public information campaign to help Cedar Rapids voters understand what a "yes" vote means, what it does not mean, and what the path forward would look like. Counter misinformation from opposition (Alliant will likely fund an opposition campaign). Voter outreach and door-to-door voter turnout efforts in November 2027.

Phase 5 : November 2027

Election Day, Cedar Rapids Voters Decide

Cedar Rapids residents vote on whether to authorize the city to pursue acquisition of the local electric distribution system from Alliant Energy and establish a municipal utility. A "yes" vote is a mandate to begin formal negotiations and IUB proceedings, not an immediate takeover.

Phase 6 : 2028 and Beyond (if voters approve)

Acquisition & Transition

If the referendum passes, the city begins formal acquisition proceedings through the Iowa Utilities Board. Negotiations with Alliant, IUB review, municipal bond financing, and transition planning. Based on comparable municipalization efforts nationally, this phase typically takes 2–5 years. Cedar Rapids would begin operating its own utility with locally hired staff, local governance, and local accountability.

⚠️ Timeline Note

This timeline is subject to change. How Alliant responds, what legal or regulatory obstacles arise, and how quickly we build community support will all affect the pace. We will adapt as needed and keep this page updated.

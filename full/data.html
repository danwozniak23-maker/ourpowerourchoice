Alliant's Stranded Asset Cost Record in Iowa

When a utility builds or buys an asset and then retires it early, those costs are often passed to ratepayers as "stranded costs", you pay for their business decisions long after the plant stops running.

Sixth Street Generating Station

Alliant shut down the Sixth Street plant in Cedar Rapids in 2023 but continues to collect $216 million from Iowa customers through 2037. This was approved by the Iowa Utilities Board as part of a settlement in rate case proceedings.

Iowa Municipal Utility Case Studies

These cities made the same decision Cedar Rapids is now considering, and have been running successfully for decades.

Cedar Falls Utilities

  • City-owned electric, gas, water, and broadband utility
  • Consistently among Iowa's lowest residential electric rates
  • Operates Cedar Falls Community TeleCom (broadband) alongside electric service
  • Profits reinvested locally, no out-of-state shareholders
  • Population comparable to Cedar Rapids neighborhoods

Ames Electric Services

  • City-owned since 1890, over 130 years of continuous municipal operation
  • Nationally recognized for reliability and low rates
  • Has led Iowa cities in renewable energy integration
  • Governed by Ames City Council, local accountability
  • Iowa State University as anchor customer, similar to CR data center dynamic

Muscatine Power & Water

  • Frequently cited as Iowa's lowest residential electric rates
  • City-owned since early 1900s
  • Operates electric, water, and internet services
  • Revenue surplus used for community projects and infrastructure
  • Often used as the benchmark in Iowa rate comparisons

📚 National Context

There are approximately 2,000 municipal electric utilities operating in the United States. According to the American Public Power Association (APPA), public power customers on average pay lower rates than customers of investor-owned utilities.

Recent successful municipalization campaigns include Boulder, CO (2011–2020 effort, ultimately settled with Xcel Energy for significant concessions) and Winter Park, FL (acquired Florida Power & Light's local network in 2005, with rates and reliability improving measurably). Each city's path is unique, Cedar Rapids' path through Iowa law will require its own analysis.

⚖️ Iowa Legal Framework

Iowa Code Chapter 388 governs municipal utilities. We need a full legal analysis of the specific requirements for Cedar Rapids to acquire Alliant's distribution infrastructure, including Iowa Code § 476.1 on IUB jurisdiction.

💰 Acquisition Cost Estimate

The biggest unknown: what would it cost Cedar Rapids to acquire Alliant's local distribution network? This requires a rate base analysis, depreciated asset valuation, and negotiation scenario modeling.

📋 IUB Approval Process

A municipalization would require Iowa Utilities Board approval. We need to map the exact regulatory pathway, timeline, and requirements including notice, hearings, and the IUB's "public convenience and necessity" standard.

🗳️ Ballot Initiative Requirements

Cedar Rapids City Charter and Iowa Code requirements for a ballot referendum on municipal utility acquisition, including exact signature thresholds, timeline, and any required council action before a public vote.
